The problem

Four people around a table with four microphones means each mic picks up all four voices. The room hum, the chair creak and the sniff during someone else's answer are all recorded four times over, and they stack.

The fix has always been manual: scrub the timeline, find who is speaking, cut around them, mute the rest. On a two-hour episode that is an afternoon of work that requires attention but no thought.

How you use it

  1. Open the panel Window → Extensions → Hush, on the sequence you are already editing.
  2. Tick which tracks are microphones Hush reads the sequence and lists them. Name them if you like.
  3. Say what kind of recording it is Mics close together? People moving around? Three checkboxes, in plain English, no decibel numbers to guess at.
  4. Run it You get a new sequence with the cuts applied, and a plain-English report of what happened on each mic. Your original is untouched.

Built on measurement, not vibes

Hush decides using loudness, how much each pair of microphones agrees with the other, and — where the recording allows it — which mic the sound reached first. Sound takes about 3 milliseconds to travel a metre, so the mic nearest the speaker hears them fractionally sooner. That is often the only thing that separates two people sitting close together when one has a much louder voice.

It also handles the awkward realities: microphones that move mid-session, recorders whose clocks drift apart, and files that roll over at 4 GB partway through a long recording.

Every change to the detection has been scored against hand-labelled recordings, tracking two failure modes separately — words lost and leakage let through. They are never averaged into one number, because a tool that hides one behind the other is the thing that ruins an edit.

Questions

What kind of recordings does it work best on?

Best: everyone's microphone recorded into one device — a Zoom, Rodecaster, Sound Devices or similar multichannel recorder — or separate files that started at the same moment. That is where Hush can use arrival time, and it makes a large difference.

Hardest: separate recorders started at different times, with the microphones very close together. On one such session a mic picked up its neighbour louder than its own owner, and the recorders were not sample-locked, so neither loudness nor timing could separate them. Hush still improves it — around 8% of words lost and 33% leakage — but it will not be transformative. That is physics rather than a bug, and no setting fixes it.

The 5-minute free mode exists precisely so you can find out which of these your recordings are before paying.

Will it cut out words?

It is deliberately tuned to leave a mic open rather than risk clipping the start of a word — losing speech is far more damaging to an edit than letting a little leakage through. You can push it either way per microphone with a "pick up more / pick up less" control.

After every run, Hush reports what happened on each microphone in plain English, and flags any mic that ended up open unusually often or rarely.

Does it change my original sequence?

No. Hush builds a new sequence with the cuts applied and leaves yours exactly as it was. If you dislike the result, delete it.

One thing to know: because the new sequence is created by rebuilding it as a file, Premiere's importer sets its Color Setup to "Custom" with a blank output colour space, which can make the new sequence preview darker. Exports are unaffected — verified by comparing rendered frames. Setting Sequence Settings → Color Management → Color Setup to match your original fixes the preview, and Hush reminds you on the panel after every run.

How is this different from AutoPod?

AutoPod is a broader multi-camera and audio suite. Hush does one thing — deciding who is speaking and muting everyone else — and the whole design is built around getting that decision right, including using arrival time between microphones, which is unusual in this category.

If you need multi-camera switching, AutoPod does more. If the audio is the part costing you an afternoon, Hush is aimed squarely at it.
